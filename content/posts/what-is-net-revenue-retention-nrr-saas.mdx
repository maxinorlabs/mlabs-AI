Net Revenue Retention is one of the most predictive metrics in SaaS. It tells you whether your existing customer base is growing, shrinking, or holding flat without a single new sale. Investors at every stage from seed to IPO use it as a primary filter because it reveals the underlying health of the business model beneath the new ARR numbers.

If you are building a recurring revenue business and have not made NRR your north star metric yet, this guide covers everything: what it is, how to calculate it, what good looks like by stage in 2026, and how to improve it.

## What Is Net Revenue Retention?

Net Revenue Retention (NRR), also called Net Dollar Retention (NDR), is the percentage of recurring revenue retained from a cohort of existing customers over a defined period, after accounting for churn, downgrades, upgrades, and expansion.

The key word is net. NRR captures both the revenue you lose and the revenue you gain from the same group of customers. This is what separates it from Gross Revenue Retention (GRR), which only measures what you keep.

**NRR above 100%**: your existing customer base generates more revenue this period than last. You would grow even if you stopped selling entirely.

**NRR below 100%**: your existing customers are worth less this period than last. Every new customer you acquire is fighting a headwind from your own base.

## The NRR Formula

NRR is calculated over a fixed period, typically 12 months:

**NRR = (Starting ARR + Expansion ARR - Churned ARR - Contracted ARR) / Starting ARR x 100**

### Worked Example

You start the year with Rs 1 crore in ARR from 50 existing customers.

- Customers who cancelled: Rs 8 lakh lost
- Customers who downgraded to a lower tier: Rs 3 lakh lost
- Customers who upgraded or expanded: Rs 18 lakh gained
- Ending ARR from the same 50 customers: Rs 1.07 crore

**NRR = Rs 1.07 crore / Rs 1 crore x 100 = 107%**

That 107% means your existing base grew 7% on its own, without a single new customer.

## NRR vs Gross Revenue Retention: The Key Difference

| Metric | What It Measures | Can It Exceed 100%? | Captures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) | Revenue kept, excluding expansion | No | Churn and contraction only |
| Net Revenue Retention (NRR) | Revenue kept including expansion | Yes | Churn, contraction, AND expansion |

GRR tells you how good you are at keeping customers. NRR tells you whether those customers grow with you.

A company with 95% GRR and 115% NRR is keeping almost all its customers and driving strong expansion. A company with 80% GRR and 85% NRR has both a churn problem and a weak expansion motion. Track both metrics separately because a high NRR can mask a serious churn problem if expansion is unusually strong.

## NRR Benchmarks by Stage in 2026

What counts as good NRR depends on your stage, business model, and customer segment. These benchmarks draw on data from OpenView Partners, Bessemer Venture Partners, and operator experience across SaaS businesses.

| Stage | Median NRR | Top Quartile | Investor Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed to Seed | 80-90% | 95-105% | Less scrutiny; focus is on product |
| Series A | 90-100% | 105-115% | 100% is the floor expectation |
| Series B | 95-110% | 110-120% | Expansion motion required |
| Growth / Pre-IPO | 105-115% | 120-140% | Compounding base is the story |

### Benchmarks by Customer Segment

SMB-focused SaaS typically sees median NRR of 85 to 95% because small business churn is structurally higher. Mid-market SaaS clusters at 95 to 105%. Enterprise SaaS reaches 105 to 120% because large contracts renew slowly and expand meaningfully when value is demonstrated.

For reference: Snowflake reported 158% NRR at IPO. Twilio hit 133% NRR at Series B. Veeva Systems sustained 120%+ NRR across its public company life. These are exceptional outliers, but they illustrate what is possible when product architecture and customer success are designed for expansion from day one.

## Why Investors Weight NRR More Than New ARR

The reason comes down to compounding.

Consider two businesses, both at Rs 10 crore ARR, both generating Rs 30 lakh per month in new ARR.

**Business A at 85% NRR**: loses Rs 1.25 crore from existing customers every year. New ARR of Rs 3.6 crore partially offsets that. Net growth is Rs 2.35 crore per year.

**Business B at 115% NRR**: gains Rs 1.5 crore from existing customers every year without any new sales. Add Rs 3.6 crore in new ARR and net growth is Rs 5.1 crore.

Same new ARR engine. More than double the business outcome. After five years, Business A's original customer base has shrunk to 59% of its starting value. Business B's has grown to 161%.

This is the compounding logic investors are underwriting when they check NRR first. ## The Five Drivers of NRR

Every NRR number is the output of five underlying variables. Knowing which one is driving your result tells you exactly where to intervene.

**1. Logo churn rate.** The percentage of customers who cancel entirely. Even 2% monthly logo churn produces 22% annual churn, which makes strong NRR mathematically very difficult.

**2. Contraction rate.** Customers who stay but reduce their spend. Often more insidious than logo churn because it is slower and harder to see until it accumulates into a material drag on NRR.

**3. Expansion rate.** Revenue growth from existing customers through upgrades, seat additions, new modules, or usage growth. This is the variable that can push NRR above 100%.

**4. Time to first expansion.** How quickly does a customer reach the point where they naturally want more of your product? A product that delivers clear value in 30 days creates expansion opportunities in month 3 or 4. A 6-month time-to-value pushes expansion to month 9 at the earliest.

**5. Customer health and adoption depth.** Customers who use the product deeply and derive measurable value from it churn at 3 to 5 times lower rates than shallow users, and are significantly more likely to expand.

## How to Improve NRR: The Four Levers

### Lever 1: Reduce Logo Churn

Fix the onboarding. The majority of churn decisions are made in the first 60 to 90 days, usually because the customer never reached a meaningful outcome with the product. A structured onboarding playbook with defined milestones and a clear time-to-value target directly reduces early-stage churn.

### Lever 2: Protect and Recover Contracting Accounts

Build a customer health scoring system that identifies at-risk accounts 60 to 90 days before their renewal decision. Proactive intervention on at-risk accounts, before the customer is already in a cancellation mindset, is significantly more effective than reactive save attempts after the decision is made.

### Lever 3: Build a Systematic Expansion Motion

If your product and pricing are not designed for expansion, NRR above 100% is structurally very difficult. Expansion can take the form of seat-based pricing that scales with customer headcount, usage-based pricing where more usage means more revenue, or tiered pricing with clear upgrade paths tied to value milestones.

The expansion motion requires both the right product architecture and a trained customer success team that identifies expansion signals and initiates the conversation at the right moment.

### Lever 4: Drive Product Adoption Depth

Feature adoption depth is the leading indicator of both renewal and expansion. Customers who use core features regularly churn at dramatically lower rates. Tracking which features are used, by how many users, and with what frequency gives you the product roadmap signal and the customer health signal simultaneously.

## NRR Benchmarks for Indian B2B Startups: What Is Different

Global NRR benchmarks need adjustment for the Indian B2B context.

SME concentration is higher in India. A large share of Indian B2B customers are small businesses with inherently higher churn risk. SME-focused products typically see NRR of 80 to 90% even with strong customer success investment.

Expansion in Indian enterprise requires navigating procurement cycles and budget reapproval processes that can extend the expansion timeline by 2 to 3 quarters compared to US enterprise. Expansion motions need to account for this.

Early retention in Indian markets is often driven by the founder's personal relationships rather than product value and customer success process. When those relationships do not scale, retention drops. ## FAQ

### What is a good NRR for a SaaS startup?

A good NRR at Series A stage is 100% or above. At Series B, 110% is the benchmark for strong businesses. NRR above 120% indicates best-in-class expansion and retention, typical of top-quartile enterprise SaaS. Below 90% at Series A stage is a red flag requiring active intervention.

### What is the difference between NRR and churn rate?

Churn rate measures the percentage of customers or revenue you lose in a period. NRR is a net metric: it measures what you keep minus what you lose, plus what you gain from expansion. A high NRR can coexist with moderate churn if expansion revenue exceeds lost revenue. A low churn rate alone does not guarantee high NRR if there is no expansion motion.

### Can NRR exceed 100%?

Yes. NRR exceeds 100% when expansion revenue from existing customers exceeds revenue lost to churn and contraction. This is the defining characteristic of the strongest SaaS businesses: their existing customer base grows in value each year without requiring new customers to offset losses.

### How often should NRR be calculated?

Most SaaS businesses calculate NRR on a trailing 12-month basis, measuring what happened to a cohort of customers over the past year. Some track monthly for leading indicator purposes. The 12-month calculation is the standard benchmark investors use in due diligence.

### What is the difference between NRR and NDR?

NRR (Net Revenue Retention) and NDR (Net Dollar Retention) are the same metric. Different companies and investors use different terminology but the formula and meaning are identical. NRR is more common in European and Indian SaaS contexts; NDR is more common in US SaaS contexts.

### What causes low NRR in SaaS companies?

The most common causes are: high early churn from poor onboarding, contraction from customers downgrading when value is not clearly demonstrated, no expansion motion in the product or pricing model, ICP drift where the company acquired poor-fit customers to hit revenue targets, and lack of a proactive customer success function.

### How does NRR affect SaaS valuation?

NRR is a primary input into SaaS valuation. Companies with NRR above 120% typically command revenue multiples 30 to 50% higher than comparable companies with NRR below 100%, because investors are underwriting the growth of the existing base, not just the acquisition engine.
